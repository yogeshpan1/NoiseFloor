Four hypotheses, tested against a decade of GDELT event records. Each asks a structural question about how India and China narrate Nepal.

H1  ✓ CONFIRMED SIGNIFICANT

The India–China tone gap is structural

+1.85tone points

Across all three crises India's coverage of Nepal runs 1.85 tone points more negative than China's. A paired t-test (t = −13.95, n = 3) rejects the null of no difference at p = 0.0051. This is not noise — it is a fixed editorial posture.

p-value = 0.0051
H2  — NO CHANGE NOT SIG.

India's tone has not moved in ten years

-2.33mean, 2015→2025

From the 2015 earthquake to the 2025 Gen-Z protests India's mean tone barely shifts (−2.26 → −2.43 → −2.30). A regression on time yields p = 0.81 — statistically indistinguishable from a flat line. The critical baseline is frozen.

p-value = 0.81
H3  ✕ ZERO CORR. NOT SIG.

The two countries react to different triggers

r = -0.02daily correlation

Day-to-day, India and China tone move independently (Pearson r = −0.0235, p = 0.88). They are not responding to the same news events — they are running parallel, disconnected narratives about the same country.

p-value = 0.8795
H4  ↘ DRIFTING SIGNIFICANT

China is drifting negative

-0.29 → -0.71across crises

China's near-neutral baseline is eroding: −0.29 (Earthquake) → −0.45 (Blockade) → −0.71 (Gen-Z). The downward trend is significant at p = 0.015 — a slow convergence toward India's critical stance.

p-value = 0.015

Summary — how to read the engine
How to read the Density Graph:

These two bell curves show the distribution of all news articles. The peak of the curve is the average tone. Because the red curve (India) sits further left (negative), Indian news was generally much more negative than Chinese news (cyan, closer to 0). Click RUN TEST and the curves shift to the exact data for the crisis you selected.

What are the "Metrics"?
  • Tone Divergence: how positive or negative the news is (the mood of coverage).
  • Event Frequency: how much news is being published (the volume of articles).
How to read the Statistical Matrix (HX-001 to HX-004):

A Null Hypothesis (H₀) is a default assumption. A P-Value under 0.05 means the assumption is WRONG (Rejected). Here's what we tested:

  • HX-001 [Tone]: Assumption: India and China have the same average tone. REJECTED. (Fact: India is much more negative.)
  • HX-002 [Stability]: Assumption: India's tone changed between 2015 and 2025. STABLE. (Fact: India's tone has stayed exactly the same over 10 years.)
  • HX-003 [Correlation]: Assumption: Indian and Chinese media do NOT copy each other. CONFIRMED. (Fact: when Indian news panics, Chinese news doesn't react — they cover different things.)
  • HX-004 [Secular Regression]: Assumption: China's overall tone is not changing. REJECTED. (Fact: China is slowly drifting more critical over time.)

In plain English: China's news about Nepal is consistently warmer than India's; India's tone has been frozen for a decade while China's slowly sours; and day-to-day the two run parallel, disconnected narratives about the same country.

A beginner-friendly guide to how we collect data and calculate the results — plus the caveats that keep us honest.

Pipeline, step by step
01

Extraction

22,000 GDELT parquet files (35.5M events worldwide) are filtered down to every event mentioning Nepal. This leaves 148,180 relevant records.

02

Actor Attribution

Each record's source country is resolved via GDELT's Actor fields and domain geolocation, separating Indian media from Chinese media from the other 94 nations.

03

Tone Aggregation

GDELT scores each article's emotional tone from −100 to +100 using NLP sentiment analysis. We average these daily per actor, producing a comparable "mood index".

04

Hypothesis Testing

Paired t-tests across crisis windows test whether the India–China tone gap is real (HX-001), stable (HX-002), uncorrelated (HX-003) and drifting (HX-004).

05

Spike & Anomaly Detection

Volume spikes are flagged against a 30-day baseline (Sept 9 = 30.99×), and tone anomalies are flagged when |z| > 2 versus each country's own 7-day rolling mean.

06

Data Structures (DSA layer)

The Date Explorer runs on a hand-built DateIndex: sorted date array + inverted map, queried by binary search in O(log n + k). The Insight Engine adds a TF-scored inverted index over verified findings for retrieval-style Q&A.

Caveats & limitations
  • GDELT's tone score is machine-generated; sarcasm and irony can fool it.
  • Event counts measure attention, not importance.
  • Only three crises were sampled — n = 3 for the paired t-test, so p-values are indicative rather than definitive.
  • Crisis windows are aligned by trigger day; different event durations can bias averages.
  • The live feed depends on the GDELT DOC API, which occasionally rate-limits (HTTP 429).
Reproducibility
  • backend/build_data.py rebuilds every dataset + asserts locked statistics before shipping.
  • backend/tests/ contains pytest suites for the pipeline and the DateIndex.
  • notebook_addendum.ipynb reproduces the reverse-direction stats in Colab.
